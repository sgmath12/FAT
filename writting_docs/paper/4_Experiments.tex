\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We evaluate the clean--robust trade-off across datasets and architectures, isolate the anchor's
contribution, and distinguish radius-allocation effects from weight averaging and AWP.
We then examine feature geometry and attack reliability.

\subsection{Experimental Settings}

We use CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 \citep{krizhevsky2009learning}, and Tiny-ImageNet-200
\citep{le2015tiny}. The natural teacher is trained on clean data for 200 epochs and then frozen
(\Cref{tab:teachers}); its clean accuracies are $95.32\%$, $77.66\%$, and $66.29\%$, respectively,
and its AA is zero on all three datasets. Unless stated otherwise, the ResNet-18 student is
initialized from that teacher and trained with AdamW, a one-cycle schedule, and 10-step PGD, with
sensitivity matching ($p=1$), weight averaging, and AWP. The main CIFAR results use 150 epochs at the
standard $\epsilon_{\mathrm{train}}=8/255$; ablations use 100 epochs and, where stated,
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{train}}=8.8/255$, and \Cref{tab:radius} sweeps the radius. Baselines run their
authors' recipes. Detailed settings are in \Cref{app:repro}.

We evaluate the final-epoch model on the full test set at $\epsilon=8/255$, reporting clean accuracy,
the APGD-CE/APGD-T cascade (AA), their mean (Avg), and their harmonic mean (NRR); a four-attack
AutoAttack validation of the 100-epoch CIFAR-100 model gives the same robust accuracy
(\Cref{app:gradmask}). Other baseline settings follow their published recipes with our evaluation
pipeline, and the distillation baselines receive our natural
teacher; published results obtained with other teachers are collected in \Cref{tab:published}.

\subsection{What Produces the Gain?}
\label{sec:components}

\paragraph{Anchor and label supervision.}
Replacing the anchor by label cross-entropy under the same base regime reduces clean and AA by
$6.69$ and $5.91$ points (\Cref{tab:controls}). Giving cross-entropy its own optimizer and schedule
narrows but does not close the gap: CFA remains ahead by $4.63$ clean and $4.45$ AA points.

Changing initialization or optimizer and schedule changes NRR by $0.22$ and $0.11$, respectively
(\Cref{tab:recipe}). At 100 epochs with the full stack, label cross-entropy reaches $57.73/26.46$
clean/AA versus CFA's $62.17/28.86$, so the gain is not carried by weight averaging and AWP. Giving
each baseline the same natural warm start and the same stack, while leaving its objective, optimizer
and schedule untouched, raises most of them --- PGD-AT by $6.00$ NRR and ARD by $6.45$ --- and none of
them reaches CFA (\Cref{tab:baselinestack}).

\paragraph{Regression and probability targets.}
Logit regression remains effective with WA and AWP. At a uniform training radius of $8.8/255$,
100-epoch logit MSE reaches $60.36/28.02$ clean/AA against feature regression's $60.42/28.42$
(\Cref{tab:regression_stack}). Together with the base-regime comparison in \Cref{sec:fragile}, this
shows that effective clean-target regression is not confined to feature space. Teacher-only KL instead
reaches $56.97/26.96$ at its best tested temperature under the full recipe (\Cref{tab:fullmetrics});
this separate comparison does not isolate the target from classifier and radius choices.

\subsection{Radius Allocation and Training Components}

\paragraph{Which signal should allocate the radius?}
\Cref{tab:allocation} holds the mean training radius fixed and changes the allocation signal under
the full recipe. Sensitivity improves clean/AA by $1.75/0.44$ over a uniform radius. To distinguish
the signal from the spread of radii, we also preserve the sensitivity rule's exact set of multipliers
but reassign them so easier examples receive larger radii. This reduces AA by $0.80$ points.
Difficulty and margin alternatives likewise have lower NRR, supporting the use of the anchor's own
input sensitivity. These are signal controls inspired by adaptive-radius methods, rather than
reproductions of their complete training algorithms.

\paragraph{Feature geometry.}
Additional base-regime diagnostics show stronger class separation under anchoring, but not smaller
relative feature displacement under a matched attack (\Cref{app:anchor_measurements}). These
earlier trained-head models provide descriptive evidence, not a causal account of the final method.

\subsection{Reliability and Scope}

The full standard AutoAttack suite (APGD-CE, APGD-T, FAB-T, and Square) reaches $28.73\%$ on the
100-epoch CIFAR-100 repeat, identical to the two-attack cascade: neither FAB-T nor Square finds a failure among
the $2873$ surviving examples, and PGD accuracy falls to zero at $64/255$ (\Cref{app:gradmask}). That
repeat gives $62.00/28.74$ clean/AA against $62.17/28.86$ for seed $0$, so two seeds do not establish
the significance of small ablation differences (\Cref{app:repro}). Teacher and student training take
$0.39$ and $2.07$ hours for the 100-epoch student setting on one RTX 5080 (\Cref{tab:compute}).
