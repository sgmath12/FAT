\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental Settings}

\paragraph{Datasets.} CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 \citep{krizhevsky2009learning} and Tiny-ImageNet-200
\citep{le2015tiny}. Random crop with 4-pixel padding and random horizontal flip; no other
augmentation.

\paragraph{Teacher.} For each dataset, a network of the same architecture as the student, trained
naturally for 200 epochs on the same data (\Cref{tab:teachers}). No adversarial training is
performed at any point in the teacher's construction.

\paragraph{Training details.} ResNet-18 throughout the main tables, WideResNet-34-10 in
\Cref{sec:wrn}. The student is initialized from the teacher and trained for 100 epochs with
AdamW at learning rate $0.021$ under a one-cycle schedule, batch size $128$. The attack is 10-step PGD
at step size $2/255$ with a training radius of $8.8/255$; \Cref{sec:radius} varies that radius.
Weight averaging ($\kappa=0.999$, from $20\%$ of the run) and an AWP proxy \citep{2020_awp}
($\gamma=0.005$, after epoch $10$) are used and ablated in \Cref{sec:components}. Every dataset
uses this identical configuration; only the dataset name and the teacher checkpoint change.

\paragraph{Comparison models.} Two groups: adversarial training methods requiring no external network
(PGD-AT, TRADES, MART, Consistency-AT, RPAT, ADR, CURE, Generalist++, ARREST), and adversarial
distillation methods, which assume an adversarially trained teacher (ARD, RSLAD, AdaAD, IGDM,
B-MTARD). Published numbers are quoted with their source; RPAT and CURE we additionally re-ran, and
the four distillation objectives we ported and ran on our own natural teacher
(\Cref{sec:natteacher}).

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Clean accuracy, FGSM, PGD-20/10/50, CW$_\infty$ and AutoAttack (standard
version) on the full test set at $\epsilon=8/255$. NRR is the harmonic mean of clean and AutoAttack
accuracy. All of our numbers are the final-epoch weight-averaged model, with no best-checkpoint
selection.

\paragraph{Group (b) is not a like-for-like comparison, and is printed to make that visible.}
Those rows come from a WideResNet-28-10 or WRN-70-16 teacher adversarially trained with generated
data---a network more expensive than the student it teaches---so their robustness originates outside
the student. \Cref{sec:natteacher} gives the same four objectives the teacher they would have
in our setting, and every one then falls below not distilling at all. The two settings assume
different inputs and are best read side by side rather than ranked.

\paragraph{Backbone caveats.} ARREST's CIFAR-10 headline is WideResNet-34-10
(\Cref{tab:w10}); the ResNet-18 row in \Cref{tab:c10} is its smaller-backbone entry. CURE
reports no AutoAttack on CIFAR-100. RPAT's WideResNet results on CIFAR-100 are PreActResNet-18. Each
cell states the backbone its source used.

\paragraph{Reproductions.} We reproduce RPAT++ to within $0.31$ AutoAttack of its published numbers on
both datasets and report our own measurement. CURE we could \emph{not} reproduce: seven runs of the
official repository fall short on \emph{different} axes---the closest on standard accuracy reaches
$86.11$ at AA $40.65$, the closest on robustness AA $45.63$ at clean $81.15$, while the published pair
has both. Computing its RGP prominence mask on absolute values moves AA by $+15.9$, which localizes the
discrepancy without closing it, so the CURE rows above are the paper's own numbers.
