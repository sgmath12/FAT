\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We test CFA across datasets and architectures, then examine the clean-feature objective and the
allocation of perturbation radii. We report the contribution of weight averaging and AWP separately.

\subsection{Experimental Settings}

We use CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 \citep{krizhevsky2009learning}, and Tiny-ImageNet-200
\citep{le2015tiny}. The natural teacher is trained on clean data for 200 epochs and then frozen
(\Cref{tab:teachers}); its clean accuracies are $95.32\%$, $77.66\%$, and $66.29\%$, respectively,
and its AA is zero on all three datasets. Unless stated otherwise, the ResNet-18 student is
initialized from that teacher and trained with AdamW, a one-cycle schedule, and 10-step PGD, with
sensitivity matching ($p=1$), weight averaging, and AWP. The main CIFAR results use 150 epochs at the
standard $\epsilon_{\mathrm{train}}=8/255$; ablations use 100 epochs and, where stated,
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{train}}=8.8/255$. The Tiny-ImageNet and WideResNet runs also train at $8.8/255$,
which \Cref{tab:radius} shows moves the operating point along the trade-off rather than improving it:
at $8/255$ CIFAR-100 gives $63.89/27.78$ against $62.17/28.86$ at $8.8/255$, so a single radius cannot
be read as tuning. Baselines run their authors' recipes. Detailed settings are in \Cref{app:repro}.

We report clean and AA accuracy, their mean (Avg), and harmonic mean (NRR) on the full test set at
$\epsilon=8/255$. Distillation baselines receive our natural teacher. Evaluation details and
results obtained with other teachers are in
\Cref{app:repro,app:gradmask,tab:published}.

\subsection{What Produces the Gain?}
\label{sec:components}

\paragraph{Anchor and label supervision.}
Replacing the anchor by label cross-entropy under the same base regime reduces clean and AA by $6.69$
and $5.91$ points, and giving cross-entropy its own optimizer and schedule narrows the gap without
closing it, leaving CFA ahead by $4.63$ clean and $4.45$ AA points (\Cref{tab:controls}).

Initialization and schedule controls change NRR by at most $0.22$ (\Cref{tab:recipe}). Even under
the full stack, label cross-entropy remains below CFA in both clean and AA accuracy. Applying the
same additions to the baselines' own recipes helps most but does not close the gap
(\Cref{tab:baselinestack}).

\paragraph{Regression and probability targets.}
Logit regression remains effective with WA and AWP: at a uniform $8.8/255$ and 100 epochs, logit MSE
reaches $60.36/28.02$ clean/AA against feature regression's $60.42/28.42$
(\Cref{tab:regression_stack}). Under the final 150-epoch recipe with sensitivity-matched radii,
logit regression reaches $64.49/27.85$ (NRR $38.90$), within $0.26/0.13$ clean/AA points of feature
regression (\Cref{tab:finalallocation}). Thus effective clean-target regression is not confined to
feature space. Teacher-only KL reaches $56.97/26.96$ at its best tested temperature under the full
recipe (\Cref{tab:fullmetrics}), a comparison that does not isolate the target from classifier and
radius choices.

\subsection{Radius Allocation and Training Components}

\paragraph{Does allocation help in the final recipe?}
Holding the teacher, training schedule, mean radius, weight averaging, and AWP fixed, replacing the
uniform feature-anchor radius with sensitivity matching raises clean accuracy by
$2.19\mathbin{\pm}0.20$ points across three paired runs, while AA changes by
$0.03\mathbin{\pm}0.23$ points (\Cref{tab:finalallocation}). The clean gain appears in every seed,
so it persists at the configuration reported in \Cref{tab:main}; the AA difference is indistinguishable
from run-to-run variation. The
concentration of the gain on clean accuracy is an empirical observation, not a consequence predicted
by the first-order allocation argument. Logit regression with sensitivity matching reaches a similar
endpoint, but its uniform-radius counterpart was not evaluated, so this row does not estimate a
radius-allocation gain for the logit objective.

\paragraph{Which signal should allocate the radius?}
At 100 epochs, we compare the sensitivity signal with alternative assignments of the same mean
budget (\Cref{tab:allocation}). Reassigning the \emph{same radii} by sample difficulty loses $0.80$
AA points relative to sensitivity, so varying radii alone does not explain the gain. Difficulty and
margin signals also have lower NRR. These are controls on the allocation signal, not reproductions of
other adaptive-radius algorithms.
