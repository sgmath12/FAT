\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Adversarial training improves worst-case accuracy but often sacrifices clean accuracy
\citep{PGD,tsipras2018robustness,TRADES}. Robust distillation can soften this trade-off, but requires
an adversarially trained teacher \citep{ard,iad,rslad,adaad,igdm}. A naturally trained teacher is
cheaper and more accurate on clean inputs, yet vulnerable to attack. What should a robust student
learn from such a teacher?

Prior methods use natural teachers' predictions or representations
\citep{lbgat,arrest,dpfat,bmtard}. We study a particularly simple answer: match the teacher's
\emph{clean feature} throughout adversarial training, with the inherited classifier fixed. The same
target drives attack generation and student learning, without a separate label loss or temperature
search. This design exposes two choices beyond the target itself: which natural checkpoint to use,
and how to allocate perturbations across examples.

\textbf{Clean Feature Anchoring} (CFA) makes the target explicit: the student backbone learns to
match one clean teacher representation even at adversarial inputs. A fixed-anchor bound relates
this loss to clean teacher fidelity and local feature variation; it does not rely on the teacher
itself being robust.

The teacher checkpoint changes what the student learns. Across two independent CIFAR-100 training
trajectories, later natural checkpoints shift students toward higher robustness and lower clean
accuracy. Moreover, label-smoothed and mixup teachers with nearly identical clean accuracy produce
students $4.18$ clean points apart. Teacher accuracy alone is therefore not a sufficient description
of the supervision a checkpoint provides.

Even with the teacher fixed, a uniform pixel radius need not perturb the anchor loss equally across
examples. We allocate a fixed batch budget inversely to the loss's input sensitivity. In the final
recipe, this changes clean accuracy from $62.40\%$ to $64.75\%$ while AA remains near $28\%$.
A separate control shows that reassigning the same radii by sample difficulty weakens the result.
The teacher determines the
reference, while radius allocation determines where to enforce it.

CFA improves the measured trade-off across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet. On CIFAR-100
with ResNet-18 it reaches $64.75\%$ clean and $27.98\%$ AA, exceeding ARREST by $1.58$ AA points
at comparable clean accuracy (\Cref{tab:main}). Target, recipe, and radius controls distinguish the
anchor's contribution from that of the training stack.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Adversarial training driven by a natural teacher's clean feature alone, with no label loss and
  no temperature, separated from the training recipe by objective, initialization, schedule and stack
  controls; one fixed target bounds clean fidelity and local feature variation together, which is why
  no balancing coefficient appears.
  \item Evidence that the teacher checkpoint sets the student's operating point rather than its
  quality: two independent trajectories move clean accuracy and robustness in opposite directions, and
  two teachers of nearly equal clean accuracy produce students $4.18$ clean points apart.
  \item A radius allocation read from the loss's own input sensitivity, which holds at the reported
  configuration and survives controls that keep the budget, and even the multiset of radii, fixed.
\end{itemize}
