\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Adversarial training improves worst-case accuracy but often sacrifices clean accuracy
\citep{PGD,tsipras2018robustness,TRADES}. Robust distillation can soften this trade-off, but requires
an adversarially trained teacher \citep{ard,iad,rslad,adaad,igdm}. A naturally trained teacher is
cheaper and more accurate on clean inputs, yet vulnerable to attack. What should a robust student
learn from such a teacher?

Many distillation methods express supervision as a predictive distribution and tune its softness or
its weight relative to labels \citep{ard,rslad,adr,bmtard}. Direct regression offers a different
view: fix what the teacher produces at the clean input and enforce that target throughout the
student's adversarial neighborhood. We study this principle with the teacher's clean feature as the
target, an inherited fixed classifier, and no label loss. This design exposes two further choices:
which natural checkpoint supplies the target, and where to allocate the perturbation budget.

\textbf{Clean Feature Anchoring} (CFA) makes the target explicit: the student backbone matches one
clean teacher representation even at adversarial inputs. Softening the teacher's predictions mainly
moves robustness, whereas direct clean-logit and feature regression improve both axes without a
temperature search. Their near-tie identifies fixed clean-target regression, rather than the choice
between logit and feature coordinates, as the broader mechanism. We use features because they retain
the representation supplied to the inherited classifier. A fixed-anchor bound then relates the CFA
loss to clean teacher fidelity and local feature variation without requiring the teacher to be robust.

The teacher checkpoint changes what the student learns. Across two independent CIFAR-100 training
trajectories, later natural checkpoints shift students toward higher robustness and lower clean
accuracy. Moreover, label-smoothed and mixup teachers with nearly identical clean accuracy produce
students $4.18$ clean points apart. Teacher accuracy alone is therefore not a sufficient description
of the supervision a checkpoint provides.

Even with the teacher fixed, a uniform pixel radius need not perturb the anchor loss equally across
examples. We allocate a fixed batch budget inversely to the loss's input sensitivity. Across three
runs of the final recipe, this changes clean accuracy from $62.63\%$ to $64.82\%$ while AA remains
at $28.0\%$.
A separate control shows that reassigning the same radii by sample difficulty weakens the result.
The teacher determines the
reference, while radius allocation determines where to enforce it.

CFA improves the measured trade-off across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet. On CIFAR-100
with ResNet-18 it reaches $64.82\%$ clean and $28.04\%$ AA on average, the highest AutoAttack accuracy and NRR
among twelve baselines run in the same framework (\Cref{tab:main}). Target, recipe, and radius controls distinguish the
anchor's contribution from that of the training stack.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A controlled comparison from softened predictive targets to direct clean-target regression,
  instantiated as label-free CFA; for this feature realization, one anchor jointly bounds clean
  teacher fidelity and local variation.
  \item Evidence that the teacher checkpoint sets the student's operating point rather than its
  quality: two independent trajectories move clean accuracy and robustness in opposite directions, and
  two teachers of nearly equal clean accuracy produce students $4.18$ clean points apart.
  \item A radius allocation read from the loss's own input sensitivity, which holds at the reported
  configuration and survives controls that keep the budget, and even the multiset of radii, fixed.
\end{itemize}
