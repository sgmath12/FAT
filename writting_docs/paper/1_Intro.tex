\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Adversarial training improves worst-case accuracy but often sacrifices clean accuracy
\citep{PGD,tsipras2018robustness,TRADES}. Robust distillation can soften this trade-off, but requires
an adversarially trained teacher \citep{ard,iad,rslad,adaad,igdm}. A naturally trained teacher is
cheaper and more accurate on clean inputs, yet vulnerable to attack. What should a robust student
learn from such a teacher?

Prior methods use natural teachers' predictions or representations
\citep{lbgat,arrest,dpfat,bmtard}. We study a particularly simple answer: match the teacher's
\emph{clean feature} throughout adversarial training, with the inherited classifier fixed. The same
target drives attack generation and student learning, without a separate label loss or temperature
search. This design exposes two choices beyond the target itself: which natural checkpoint to use,
and how to allocate perturbations across examples.

\textbf{Clean Feature Anchoring} (CFA) makes the target explicit: the student backbone learns to
match one clean teacher representation even at adversarial inputs. A fixed-anchor bound relates
this loss to clean teacher fidelity and local feature variation; it does not rely on the teacher
itself being robust.

The choice of teacher is less straightforward than its clean accuracy suggests. Along the CIFAR-100
training trajectory, the teacher gains $2.51$ clean points while its student's clean accuracy falls
by $2.04$ and robustness improves. Two teachers with nearly identical clean accuracy, trained with
label smoothing and mixup, produce students $4.18$ clean points apart. These comparisons motivate
examining teacher properties beyond accuracy, rather than simply taking the final checkpoint.

Even with the teacher fixed, a uniform pixel radius need not perturb the anchor loss equally across
examples. We allocate a fixed batch budget inversely to the loss's input sensitivity, then test the
allocation against a control that reassigns the \emph{same} radii to different examples. Teacher
selection determines the reference; radius allocation determines where to enforce it.

CFA improves the measured trade-off across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet. On CIFAR-100
with ResNet-18 it reaches $64.75\%$ clean and $27.98\%$ AA, exceeding ARREST by $1.58$ AA points
at comparable clean accuracy (\Cref{tab:main}). Target, recipe, and radius controls distinguish the
anchor's contribution from that of the training stack.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A single clean-feature target, with a bound relating teacher fidelity and local variation.
  \item Evidence that teacher clean accuracy alone does not determine student clean accuracy.
  \item Sensitivity-based radius allocation, tested with fixed-budget controls across three datasets.
\end{itemize}
