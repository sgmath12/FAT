\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Clean Feature Anchoring}
\label{sec:anchor}

Clean feature anchoring (CFA) turns the clean representation studied in \Cref{sec:analysis} into the
training target. Let $\Phi_t$ and $h_t$ denote the frozen feature map and classifier of the natural
teacher. The student inherits both components, $\Phi_s \leftarrow \Phi_t$ and $h_s=h_t$, after which
only $\Phi_s$ is optimized. For a clean example $x$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\mathrm{anc}}(x';x)
&= \big\lVert \Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x) \big\rVert_2^2,
\qquad &
x_{\mathrm{adv}}
&= \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x'\in B(x,\epsilon)}\ell_{\mathrm{anc}}(x';x),\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CFA}}
&= \mathbb{E}_x\!\left[\ell_{\mathrm{anc}}(x_{\mathrm{adv}};x)\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eqn:anchor}
\end{equation}
The target $\Phi_t(x)$ is evaluated once at the clean input and held fixed while the adversarial
example is generated. We approximate the inner maximum using $K$ projected gradient-ascent steps,
\begin{equation}
x^{(k+1)}
= \Pi_{B(x,\epsilon)}\!\left(
  x^{(k)} + a\,\mathrm{sign}\!\left[
  \nabla_{x^{(k)}}\ell_{\mathrm{anc}}(x^{(k)};x)\right]\right),
\qquad x^{(0)}=x+\eta,
\label{eqn:pgd}
\end{equation}
where $\eta$ is a random start and $\Pi$ projects onto the perturbation set. Thus, adversarial-example
generation and backbone training use the same feature discrepancy. At inference the student is
$h_t\circ\Phi_s$; the classifier remains frozen, and training uses neither a label loss nor a logit
loss.

\Cref{eqn:anchor} specifies the feature-space target but still assigns every example the same
pixel-space radius $\epsilon$. Because an equal input displacement need not induce an equal change in
$\ell_{\mathrm{anc}}$, we next allocate the fixed perturbation budget according to the local input
sensitivity of this loss.

\subsection{Sensitivity-Matched $\epsilon$}
\label{sec:angeps}

Under a uniform radius, the local sensitivity of the anchor varies substantially across examples
(\Cref{app:box}). We therefore choose a per-sample radius that approximately equalizes the
first-order change in the anchor loss while preserving the total perturbation budget. For an example
$x_i$, define its clean-point sensitivity as
\begin{equation}
g_i
= \left.\left\lVert\nabla_{x'}\ell_{\mathrm{anc}}(x';x_i)\right\rVert_1\right|_{x'=x_i},
\qquad
\max_{\lVert \delta \rVert_\infty \le e}
\left\langle
\left.\nabla_{x'}\ell_{\mathrm{anc}}(x';x_i)\right|_{x'=x_i},\delta
\right\rangle
\;=\; e\,g_i .
\label{eqn:firstorder}
\end{equation}
The equality follows from the duality of $\ell_\infty$ and $\ell_1$.
The predicted loss change under radius $\epsilon_i$ is therefore $\epsilon_i g_i$. Equalizing this
quantity across a minibatch of $N$ examples amounts to imposing
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i \, g_i \;=\; c \quad \text{for all } i ,
\qquad \text{subject to} \qquad
\sum_{i=1}^{N} \epsilon_i \;=\; N \epsilon .
\label{eqn:equalize}
\end{equation}
The constraint holds the total attack budget fixed, which is what makes the rule comparable to a
uniform radius at equal attack strength.
Solving \Cref{eqn:equalize} gives $c = N\epsilon \big/ \sum_j g_j^{-1}$ and hence
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i \;=\; N\epsilon \cdot \frac{g_i^{-1}}{\sum_j g_j^{-1}} ,
\qquad\text{generalized to}\qquad
\epsilon_i \;\propto\; g_i^{-p} ,
\label{eqn:angeps_ideal}
\end{equation}
where $p = 0$ recovers the uniform radius and $p = 1$ gives the full equalization above.
The allocation of \Cref{eqn:angeps_ideal} is equivalently the max--min solution, because moving budget
from the example whose objective moves most to the example whose objective moves least improves the
worst case until no such move remains.
Our implementation reads the gradient in $\ell_2$ rather than in the $\ell_1$ derived here, for the
reason given in \Cref{app:box}, and the two norms differ by $0.29$ points of AutoAttack accuracy.
What the rule gains over a uniform radius is preserved in direction under either norm and is larger on
the clean axis than on the robust one, and \Cref{app:box} reports both settings.

\paragraph{Keeping the radii in range.}
\Cref{eqn:angeps_ideal} assigns unbounded radii where $g_i \to 0$. In particular, the clean anchor
discrepancy is zero for every sample immediately after the student is initialized from the teacher, so
the allocation begins uniformly and differentiates as training proceeds.
We therefore constrain $\epsilon_i / \epsilon \in [0.5, 1.5]$ and solve the budget constraint inside
that box, which \Cref{app:box} states exactly and which reduces to restoring the mean when no clip is
active.
We use $p = 1$ throughout and never tune it, and \Cref{tab:allocation} reports the $p = 0$ endpoint
under the same recipe.

\paragraph{Relation to existing per-sample radii.}
Assigning a different $\epsilon$ to each example is not new, as IAAT \citep{iaat}, MMA \citep{mma} and
CAT \citep{cat} all do so.
These methods assign the radius from the difficulty of the example or from its margin in input space,
whereas \Cref{eqn:angeps_ideal} assigns it from the input sensitivity of the training loss, which is
the geometry the objective is written in.
The difference is not one of dispersion, since all three signals are dispersed on the shipped recipe
and ours is in fact the least dispersed of them (\Cref{app:box}).
What separates the rules is the geometry along which the budget is spread, and the experiments in
\Cref{sec:exp} therefore compare competing allocations rather than an allocation against its
absence.%

The distinction can be tested without confounding it with the budget.
We hold the exact multiset of weights that \Cref{eqn:angeps_ideal} produces, so that the values, the
clip and the mean are unchanged, and only reassign which example receives which weight in the order
that IAAT and CAT would use.
The reassignment alone costs $0.80$ points of AutoAttack accuracy and falls below the uniform
baseline on AutoAttack, CW and NRR alike.
Replacing the signal outright by difficulty magnitude or by the logit margin of MMA is likewise worse
than allocating nothing (\Cref{tab:allocation}).
Among the signals we tested, only the input sensitivity of the loss improves on a uniform radius.

\Cref{alg:cfa} summarizes both training stages. The natural teacher is the only component trained with
labels; the anchored stage introduces no loss weight, temperature, or auxiliary head.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
\Cref{eqn:firstorder} is derived for the $\ell_1$ norm while our runs read the gradient in $\ell_2$,
and the gap between the two is small but larger than our seed spread, as reported in \Cref{app:box}.
The sensitivity $g_i$ is also a single backward pass at the clean $x_i$, so it measures the sensitivity
at the starting point of the attack rather than along its trajectory.
