\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Clean Feature Anchoring}
\label{sec:anchor}

Let $\Phi_t$ be the frozen feature map of a network of the same architecture trained naturally on the
same data, and let $h_t$ be its classifier. The student's feature map is initialized at
$\Phi_s \leftarrow \Phi_t$ and trained with
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_x \big\lVert \Phi_s(x_{\mathrm{adv}}) - \Phi_t(x) \big\rVert^2 ,
\qquad
x_{\mathrm{adv}} \;=\; \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x' \in B(x,\epsilon)}
  \big\lVert \Phi_s(x') - \Phi_t(x) \big\rVert ,
\label{eqn:anchor}
\end{equation}
where the inner maximization is realized by $K$ steps of projected gradient ascent on the same
quantity the outer problem minimizes,
\begin{equation}
x^{(k+1)} \;=\; \Pi_{B(x,\epsilon)}\!\left(
  x^{(k)} + a \cdot \mathrm{sign}\, \nabla_{x}
  \big\lVert \Phi_s(x^{(k)}) - \Phi_t(x) \big\rVert^2 \right),
\qquad x^{(0)} = x + \eta ,
\label{eqn:pgd}
\end{equation}
with $\eta$ a small random initialization and $\Pi$ the projection onto the ball. Inner and outer
problems therefore agree, as they do in standard adversarial training and unlike methods whose attack
is generated against a different objective from the one being trained.

\Cref{eqn:anchor} is the \emph{entire} objective for the backbone. No label term, no logit term, and
no coefficient joining them. Three properties follow from that form, and each is a statement about the
construction rather than an empirical observation.

\paragraph{(i) The teacher never enters the perturbation ball.} In both \Cref{eqn:anchor} and
\Cref{eqn:pgd} the teacher is evaluated at $x$ alone. Formally, the objective is a functional of
$\Phi_t$ only through the single vector $\Phi_t(x)$, so two teachers agreeing at $x$ and differing
arbitrarily on $B(x,\epsilon) \setminus \{x\}$ induce the same optimization problem. The teacher's
$63.8^\circ$ of rotation under attack is exactly such a difference, and it is therefore invisible to
training. The converse holds by the same argument: a quantity the objective cannot see cannot be a
source of robustness either.

\paragraph{(ii) The anchor is exactly zero at the clean point at initialization.} Since
$\Phi_s \leftarrow \Phi_t$, we have $\lVert \Phi_s(x) - \Phi_t(x) \rVert = 0$ for every $x$, so the loss
is nonzero only because of the perturbation: every gradient the backbone receives at the start of
training is attributable to the attack, which is the quantity to be trained. A logit target has this
property only at temperature $1$, where it carries no dark knowledge. It also has a consequence used
in \Cref{sec:angeps}: at the first step the per-sample sensitivities are identical, so the radius
allocation begins uniform and differentiates only as the student moves away from the teacher.

\paragraph{(iii) The classifier is inherited, and this is what a feature target buys.} At test time
the student is $h_t \circ \Phi_s$: the head is never trained. This is available because the target is
written on the feature. A loss written on logits constrains only the composition $W\Phi$, so it
determines the pair $(\Phi, W)$ up to the gauge
\begin{equation}
(\Phi, W) \;\longmapsto\; (A\Phi,\; W A^{-1}), \qquad A \in \mathrm{GL}(d),
\label{eqn:gauge}
\end{equation}
and the same logits are reachable from a continuum of representations, none of which the teacher's
classifier is calibrated for. Fixing the target at the feature removes that freedom and pins $\Phi_s$
to $\Phi_t$ itself, so $h_t$ remains the right classifier for it. \Cref{tab:ablation} shows this is not
merely permissible but preferred: every alternative we tried, including the best head temperature,
scores below leaving the classifier alone. It is also what removes the last two hyperparameters, since
a head distillation term would reintroduce a temperature and a weight.

\subsection{Sensitivity-Matched $\epsilon$}
\label{sec:angeps}

\paragraph{The problem a uniform radius creates.} \Cref{eqn:anchor} is optimized over a \emph{pixel}
ball while what it measures is a \emph{feature} displacement, and the two are not proportional. The
same $\epsilon$ moves the objective by an amount that varies across the dataset, and by a lot: logged
over training, the coefficient of variation of $\lVert \nabla_x \mathcal{L} \rVert$ across a minibatch
is $0.64$. A fixed radius therefore delivers a strongly non-uniform amount of pressure---some examples
are attacked hard in the geometry the loss lives in, others barely at all---even though every example
is allotted the same pixel budget. Nothing in \Cref{eqn:anchor} asks for that.

\paragraph{Equalizing the movement.} Inside an $\ell_\infty$ ball of radius $e$ the first-order change
of any differentiable loss is
\begin{equation}
\max_{\lVert \delta \rVert_\infty \le e} \langle \nabla_x \mathcal{L}, \delta \rangle
\;=\; e \, \lVert \nabla_x \mathcal{L} \rVert_1 ,
\label{eqn:firstorder}
\end{equation}
since $\ell_1$ is the dual norm of $\ell_\infty$. Writing $g_i = \lVert \nabla_x \mathcal{L}(x_i)
\rVert$, the movement induced at example $i$ by a radius $\epsilon_i$ is $\epsilon_i g_i$ to first
order, so equalizing it across a minibatch of $N$ examples means imposing
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i \, g_i \;=\; c \quad \text{for all } i ,
\qquad \text{subject to} \qquad
\sum_{i=1}^{N} \epsilon_i \;=\; N \epsilon .
\label{eqn:equalize}
\end{equation}
The constraint is what makes the rule comparable to a uniform one: it holds the \emph{total} attack
budget fixed. Solving \Cref{eqn:equalize} gives $c = N\epsilon \big/ \sum_j g_j^{-1}$ and hence
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i \;=\; N\epsilon \cdot \frac{g_i^{-1}}{\sum_j g_j^{-1}} ,
\qquad\text{generalized to}\qquad
\epsilon_i \;\propto\; g_i^{-p} ,
\label{eqn:angeps_ideal}
\end{equation}
with $p = 0$ recovering the uniform radius and $p = 1$ the full equalization above. Equivalently
\Cref{eqn:angeps_ideal} is the max--min allocation: moving budget from the example whose objective
moves most to the one whose objective moves least always improves the worst case, and the optimum is
where no such move remains.

\paragraph{Keeping the radii in range.} Left alone, \Cref{eqn:angeps_ideal} assigns unbounded radii to
examples where $g_i \to 0$, which happens at the start of training by property (ii) above. We therefore
constrain $\epsilon_i / \epsilon \in [w_{\mathrm{lo}}, w_{\mathrm{hi}}] = [0.5, 1.5]$ and solve the
budget constraint inside the box: with $r_i = (\bar g / g_i)^p$, the exact solution is the scale $t$
with
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathrm{clip}\big(t\, r_i,\; w_{\mathrm{lo}},\; w_{\mathrm{hi}}\big) \;=\; N ,
\qquad
\epsilon_i \;=\; \epsilon \cdot \mathrm{clip}\big(t\, r_i,\; w_{\mathrm{lo}}, w_{\mathrm{hi}}\big) .
\label{eqn:angeps}
\end{equation}
The left side is continuous and nondecreasing in $t$ from $N w_{\mathrm{lo}}$ to $N w_{\mathrm{hi}}$,
so the root exists and is found by bisection; when no clip is active it reduces to restoring the mean.
We use $p = 1$ throughout and never tune it, and \Cref{tab:ablation} reports the surrounding values.

\paragraph{Why this is not the existing per-sample radius.} Assigning a different $\epsilon$ per
example is not new: IAAT \citep{iaat}, MMA \citep{mma} and CAT \citep{cat} all do it. They assign it
from the \emph{difficulty} of the example or from its margin in \textbf{input} space.
\Cref{eqn:angeps} assigns it from the input-sensitivity of the \textbf{training loss}---the geometry
the objective is actually written in---and the two are not the same signal. Measured on the same
batches, the difficulty score has a coefficient of variation of $0.03$ where the sensitivity has
$0.64$: allocating by difficulty is close to allocating uniformly.

The distinction is testable without confounding it with the budget. Holding the exact multiset of
weights \Cref{eqn:angeps} produces---same values, same clip, same mean, therefore the same total
attack budget---and only reassigning which example receives which, ordered by difficulty as
IAAT and CAT would, costs $0.74$ AutoAttack and lands below the uniform baseline on both CW and NRR
(\Cref{tab:ablation}). The same weights in a different order move \emph{along} the frontier; only this
order moves the frontier itself.

\Cref{alg:cfa} states the procedure in full. Training the natural teacher is the only place labels
appear and is ordinary supervised training; the anchored phase uses none, since the teacher vector
$\phi_i$ is the entire supervisory signal, read once per example at the clean input, with the
classifier carried through untouched. The method introduces no loss weight, no temperature and no
auxiliary head, and the only quantity we vary between datasets is the teacher checkpoint.

\paragraph{Caveats.} \Cref{eqn:firstorder} asks for the $\ell_1$ norm and our runs use $\ell_2$; both
were trained and agree within noise. And $g_i$ is a single backward pass at the clean $x_i$, so it is
the sensitivity at the attack's starting point rather than along its trajectory.
