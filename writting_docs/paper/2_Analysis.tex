\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

A natural teacher is attractive precisely because it retains the clean accuracy that adversarial
training tends to lose. Its predictions and representations, however, are not themselves robust.
The relevant question is therefore not how to make the teacher reliable inside the perturbation set,
but what fixed signal it can provide while robustness is learned by the student's inner
maximization. We first compare probability-space supervision with direct regression, and then
characterize the clean-feature objective suggested by this comparison. Finally, we vary the teacher
checkpoint to examine what information the resulting student actually inherits. Together, these
analyses motivate the training procedure in \Cref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Preliminaries}

Let $(x,y)\sim\mathcal{P}$, where $x\in\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^D$ is a vectorized image and
$y\in\{1,\ldots,C\}$ is its class label. For an $\ell_\infty$ perturbation radius $\epsilon$, we
write
\begin{equation}
B(x,\epsilon)=\{x'\in\mathcal{X}:\lVert x'-x\rVert_\infty\le\epsilon\}.
\label{eqn:threat_set}
\end{equation}
A network is decomposed as $f=h\circ\Phi$, where
$\Phi:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^d$ is its feature map and
$h(z)=Wz+b$ is its linear classifier. We use subscripts $t$ and $s$ for the teacher and student,
respectively. The teacher $f_t=h_t\circ\Phi_t$ is first trained on clean examples from the same
dataset and is kept fixed thereafter; the student has the same architecture. Unless stated
otherwise, all feature distances are Euclidean. Continuity of the networks and compactness of
$B(x,\epsilon)$ ensure that the maxima below are attained.

\subsection{Do Natural Teachers Need Softened Targets?}
\label{sec:fragile}

Soft predictions are a standard source of supervision in robust distillation
\citep{ard,rslad,adr,bmtard}. This consideration is especially relevant for a natural teacher,
whose output is typically much more concentrated than that of a robust model. Temperature scaling
is thus a natural first attempt at adapting its predictions to adversarial training. There are,
however, two distinct ways to introduce the temperature. Let
\begin{equation}
q_t^\tau(x)=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(f_t(x)/\tau\right),
\qquad
p_s^\tau(x')=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(f_s(x')/\tau\right).
\label{eqn:soft_targets}
\end{equation}
We compare a teacher-only formulation and conventional knowledge distillation,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{T\text{-}only}}^\tau(x,x')
=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(q_t^\tau(x)\,\|\,p_s^1(x')\right),
\qquad
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}^\tau(x,x')
=\tau^2D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(q_t^\tau(x)\,\|\,p_s^\tau(x')\right).
\label{eqn:kd_variants}
\end{equation}
The former changes only the supervisory distribution, as in methods that adjust a teacher's
temperature while leaving the student temperature fixed \citep{adr,bmtard}. The latter applies the
same temperature to both networks and includes the usual $\tau^2$ scaling
\citep{hinton2015distilling}.

This distinction matters at large temperature. In the teacher-only objective,
$q_t^\tau(x)$ converges to the uniform distribution, so excessive softening progressively removes
class-dependent information from the target. By contrast, if
$\bar f=f-C^{-1}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}f$ denotes centered logits, then
\begin{equation}
\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}^\tau(x,x')
=\frac{1}{2C}\left\lVert\bar f_t(x)-\bar f_s(x')\right\rVert_2^2.
\label{eqn:kd_limit}
\end{equation}
Shared-temperature KD therefore approaches a metric regression problem rather than a uniform-target
objective. This observation also suggests two temperature-free controls: direct regression onto the
teacher's clean logits, and regression one layer earlier onto its clean representation. The latter
gives the clean-feature anchor
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{anchor}}(x)
=\max_{x'\in B(x,\epsilon)}
\left\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\right\rVert_2^2,
\label{eqn:anchor_percell}
\end{equation}
while the logit control replaces $\Phi$ by $f$. In every case, adversarial examples maximize the
same discrepancy that is subsequently minimized.

We compare these targets on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18. The natural teacher attains $77.66\%$ clean
accuracy, and all students share its initialization, a 50-epoch schedule, and
$\epsilon=8/255$. The KL objectives update the entire student, whereas both direct-regression
controls retain the inherited classifier. To make the degree of softening explicit, we measure the
entropy of $q_t^\tau$; for $C=100$, the entropy of a uniform target is $\log C=4.61$. AA$_2$
comprises APGD-CE and APGD-T from AutoAttack. Exact values are reported in
\Cref{tab:targetcomparison_values}.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figure/analysis_targets.pdf}
  \caption{From target softening to metric regression on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18.
  (a)~The teacher-target entropy approaches that of the uniform distribution as $\tau$ increases.
  (b)~Clean--AA$_2$ trajectories for teacher-only and shared-temperature KL. Numbers annotate
  $\tau$; L and F denote fixed-head logit regression and feature anchoring, respectively.}
  \label{fig:targetcomparison}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:targetcomparison} exhibits the two limiting behaviors above. When only the teacher is
softened, robustness improves until $\tau=8$, but the clean accuracy changes little;
pushing the already near-uniform target further degrades both metrics. Shared-temperature KD avoids
this turnover and recovers more clean accuracy, but its gains saturate as it approaches logit
regression. Removing the probability parameterization altogether closes most of the remaining gap:
the feature anchor improves on logit regression by only $0.13$ clean and $0.19$ AA$_2$ points. The
experiment therefore does not imply that features are intrinsically superior to logits.
Instead, it shows that target softening improves prediction-space distillation, while direct metric
regression yields a better clean--robust balance without requiring a calibrated predictive
distribution.

\subsection{A Clean Representation as the Sole Target}
\label{sec:sole_target}

The preceding result motivates placing the regression target at the feature layer, where it can be
used directly with the teacher's classifier. We initialize $\Phi_s$ from $\Phi_t$, retain
$h_s=h_t$, and train only the student feature extractor with
\Cref{eqn:anchor_percell}. The vector $\Phi_t(x)$ is computed from the clean example and remains the
common target for every $x'\in B(x,\epsilon)$. Consequently, the inner maximization and the outer
minimization act on the same feature discrepancy, and the teacher's behavior at perturbed inputs is
absent from the objective.

The role of the representation is as important as the layer at which it is taken. ARREST
\citep{arrest}, for example, preserves a natural representation as a regularizer alongside
adversarial cross-entropy. Here the clean representation instead supplies the entire supervision for
both adversarial-example generation and backbone training. Adding a label objective would introduce
a second target and a coefficient governing its influence; the anchor is designed to make neither
necessary. The frozen classifier then converts feature alignment into predictions without an
auxiliary head objective. This construction is the particular feature-space realization we pursue,
while the logit-regression control in \Cref{fig:targetcomparison} makes clear that temperature-free
regression is the broader principle.

\subsection{What the Anchor Controls}
\label{sec:decomp}

We next characterize what is imposed by using one clean representation throughout the perturbation
set. For any $x\in\mathcal{X}$ and $\epsilon\ge0$, let $B=B(x,\epsilon)$ and define
\begin{align}
L &= \max_{x'\in B}\left\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\right\rVert_2,
\label{eqn:L}\\
F &= \left\lVert\Phi_s(x)-\Phi_t(x)\right\rVert_2,
\label{eqn:F}\\
O &= \max_{x',x''\in B}\left\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x'')\right\rVert_2.
\label{eqn:O}
\end{align}
Thus, $L^2=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{anchor}}(x)$ is the worst-case anchor loss, $F$ measures clean
fidelity to the teacher, and $O$ is the diameter of the student's representation over the
perturbation set.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-anchor decomposition]
\label{prop:decomp}
For every $x\in\mathcal{X}$ and $\epsilon\ge0$, the quantities in
\Cref{eqn:L,eqn:F,eqn:O} satisfy
\begin{equation}
L\;\le\;F+O\;\le\;3L.
\label{eqn:decomp}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any $x'\in B$, the triangle inequality gives
\begin{equation*}
\left\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\right\rVert_2
\le
\left\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x)\right\rVert_2
+\left\lVert\Phi_s(x)-\Phi_t(x)\right\rVert_2
\le O+F.
\end{equation*}
Maximizing over $x'$ yields $L\le F+O$. Conversely, $x\in B$ implies $F\le L$, and for any
$x',x''\in B$,
\begin{equation*}
\left\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x'')\right\rVert_2
\le
\left\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\right\rVert_2
+\left\lVert\Phi_s(x'')-\Phi_t(x)\right\rVert_2
\le2L.
\end{equation*}
Hence $O\le2L$, and therefore $F+O\le3L$.
\end{proof}

The proposition gives a precise, limited interpretation of the anchor. If
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{anchor}}(x)\le\delta^2$, then the student's clean feature lies within
$\delta$ of the teacher target and all of its features in the perturbation set have diameter at most
$2\delta$. A single worst-case distance therefore controls fidelity and local variation without
balancing two penalties. Notably, this conclusion requires no assumption about how the teacher itself
varies under perturbation, because only $\Phi_t(x)$ appears in the definitions.

With the inherited classifier $h_t(z)=W_tz+b_t$, the same anchor also controls prediction-space
deviation:
\begin{equation}
\left\lVert f_s(x')-f_t(x)\right\rVert_2
\le\left\lVert W_t\right\rVert_{2\rightarrow2}L,
\qquad x'\in B(x,\epsilon).
\label{eqn:head_bound}
\end{equation}
Whether this bound preserves the predicted class additionally depends on the teacher's clean margin.
Accordingly, \Cref{prop:decomp} characterizes the objective rather than proving that feature targets
outperform logit targets or that a small anchor alone guarantees robust classification; these points
are discussed further in \Cref{app:anchor_scope,app:robustness}.

\subsection{What Does the Natural Teacher Transfer?}
\label{sec:teacher_transfer}
\label{sec:geometry}

Once the clean representation becomes the sole target, the choice of teacher checkpoint is no longer
incidental. If the student merely inherited the teacher's scalar accuracy, a more accurate teacher
would be expected to produce a more accurate student. We test this expectation using five checkpoints
from a single natural-training trajectory. Only the checkpoint used for initialization, feature
targets, and classifier inheritance is changed; the student training procedure is otherwise fixed.
To track representation structure, we measure the ratio $S_w/S_b$ of within-class to between-class
scatter on unit-normalized clean features, for which a smaller value indicates stronger class
separation.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figure/teacher_checkpoint_trends.pdf}
  \caption{Teacher-checkpoint effects on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18. (a)~Accuracy changes relative to
  the 50-epoch checkpoint reveal opposing teacher-clean and student-clean trends, while student
  robustness initially rises. (b)~The class-scatter ratio decreases for both networks. Exact values
  are reported in \Cref{tab:teacherladder_values}.}
  \label{fig:teacherladder}
\end{figure}

The result contradicts accuracy-only transfer. Across the ladder, the teacher gains $2.51$ points of
clean accuracy, whereas the student loses $2.04$ points. Robust accuracy moves in the opposite
direction before saturating at the later checkpoints. Meanwhile, the class-scatter ratio decreases
monotonically for both networks, showing that changes in the teacher's representation are reflected
in the student even though their clean accuracies move in opposite directions. Additional geometric
diagnostics in \Cref{app:anchor_measurements} identify between-class separation, rather than
within-class concentration, as the component most clearly shared by the two trajectories.

We therefore view teacher training length as a control over the clean--robust operating point, not as
a proxy that should simply be maximized. The evidence supports the restrained conclusion that the
student tracks the teacher's class geometry more closely than its scalar clean accuracy; because
accuracy and geometry co-vary along this checkpoint sequence, it does not establish class separation
as the unique causal mechanism.

The analysis above specifies what the student should match, but not how strongly a fixed pixel-space
radius perturbs that target for each example. The same $\epsilon$ can induce very different changes
in a feature-space loss. \Cref{sec:method} first states the resulting clean-feature training
procedure and then derives a sample-dependent radius that allocates a fixed perturbation budget
according to the input sensitivity of the anchor.
