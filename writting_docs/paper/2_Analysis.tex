\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

A naturally trained teacher learns discriminative representations of clean inputs, yet these
representations need not be stable under perturbations. We investigate whether they can
nevertheless serve as the sole supervision for adversarial training. We first examine how the
choice between softened predictions and feature targets affects clean and robust accuracy.
The feature-based results then motivate a closer analysis of the objective: a single clean-feature
anchor constrains both agreement with the teacher and local variation of the student's
representation, providing a basis for the training scheme in \Cref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Preliminaries}

Let $x\in\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^D$ denote a vectorized image and $y\in\{1,\ldots,C\}$ its class label,
with $(x,y)\sim\mathcal{P}$. We write a network as $f=h\circ\Phi$, where
$\Phi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^d$ is the feature map and $h(z)=Wz+b$ maps features to
$C$-dimensional logits. For a perturbation radius $\epsilon\ge0$, define
$B(x,\epsilon)=\{x'\in\mathcal{X}:\lVert x'-x\rVert_\infty\le\epsilon\}$.
Standard adversarial training \citep{PGD} minimizes the worst-case cross-entropy loss over this set:
\begin{equation}
\min_\theta\ \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{P}}\!
\left[\max_{x'\in B(x,\epsilon)}\ell\big(f_\theta(x'),y\big)\right],
\label{eqn:at}
\end{equation}
where $\theta$ denotes the trainable parameters and $\ell$ is cross-entropy.

We denote the teacher and student by $f_t=h_t\circ\Phi_t$ and $f_s=h_s\circ\Phi_s$, respectively.
The teacher is trained on clean examples from the same dataset, using the same architecture as the
student, and its parameters are held fixed during distillation. Throughout the analysis, feature
distances use the Euclidean norm, and feature maps are assumed continuous with network statistics
held fixed. The maxima over the compact set $B(x,\epsilon)$ are therefore well defined.

\subsection{Choosing the Distillation Target}
\label{sec:fragile}

We begin by examining how much of the student's clean--robust accuracy trade-off can be improved
by softening the natural teacher's predictions. This is motivated by the role of soft supervision
in robust distillation \citep{rslad, adr}, including the use of predictive entropy to adjust
guidance from natural and robust teachers \citep{bmtard}. A natural teacher's predictions can be
highly concentrated, and increasing temperature redistributes probability mass toward alternative
classes. We therefore measure target entropy and maximum class probability alongside student
accuracy to examine how the two accuracy measures respond to this change in supervision.

Specifically, we use teacher targets $q_t^\tau(x)=\mathrm{softmax}(f_t(x)/\tau)$ and train the
student by minimizing
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t^\tau(x)\,\|\,\mathrm{softmax}(f_s(x_{\mathrm{adv}})))$, where $\tau>0$
is applied only to the teacher target while the student's softmax temperature remains fixed at one
across the sweep. Unlike conventional KD with a shared temperature for both distributions
\citep{hinton2015distilling}, this formulation varies the softness of the supervision without
rescaling the student's logits. The same KL loss generates adversarial examples through
maximization over $B(x,\epsilon)$ and updates the student backbone and classifier through
minimization, with no additional ground-truth label loss.

Whereas temperature scaling changes the distribution derived from the teacher's logits, feature
distillation directly supervises the representation on which its classifier operates. We therefore
consider matching $\Phi_s(x')$ to $\Phi_t(x)$ through the clean-feature anchor
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(x) \;=\; \max_{x' \in B(x,\epsilon)} \big\lVert \Phi_s(x') - \Phi_t(x) \big\rVert_2^2 .
\label{eqn:anchor_percell}
\end{equation}
In this case, adversarial examples maximize the feature distance, and only the student backbone is
updated, with the inherited classifier $h_s=h_t$ held fixed.

We compare these two forms of supervision on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 and a natural teacher
with $77.66\%$ clean accuracy.
All students are initialized from this teacher and trained for 50 epochs at $\epsilon=8/255$
with the same optimizer and schedule. Alongside student accuracy, we report the teacher targets'
maximum probability and entropy averaged over clean test inputs.

\Cref{tab:targetcomparison} shows that target softening affects clean and robust accuracy
differently. As entropy increases and the maximum class probability decreases, AA$_2$ accuracy
improves up to $\tau=8$; further softening toward a uniform target reduces this gain. Clean
accuracy, however, remains within a narrow range throughout the sweep. Thus, softer targets
improve robustness over the unsoftened target without a comparable improvement in clean accuracy.
On the other hand, feature matching achieves higher clean and AA$_2$ accuracy than every logit
configuration tested. In this comparison, directly supervising the representation therefore
offers a more favorable clean--robust accuracy balance than adjusting prediction confidence alone.

\subsection{What a Clean-Feature Anchor Controls}
\label{sec:decomp}

The preceding comparison motivates using clean features as targets; we now examine what this
choice implies for the training objective. The anchor in \Cref{eqn:anchor_percell} uses the
teacher's representation directly, without adjusting a supervisory distribution. Although it
contains only one distance, minimizing its worst-case value constrains two aspects of the student:
agreement with the teacher at the clean input and variation across perturbed inputs.
\Cref{prop:decomp} makes this connection precise by relating the anchor to clean fidelity error
and local feature variation.

Let $x\in\mathcal{X}$ be a clean input and $\epsilon\ge0$ a perturbation radius. Writing
$B=B(x,\epsilon)$ and suppressing the dependence on $x$ and $\epsilon$, define
\begin{align}
L &= \max_{x' \in B} \lVert \Phi_s(x') - \Phi_t(x) \rVert_2 , \label{eqn:L}\\
F &= \lVert \Phi_s(x) - \Phi_t(x) \rVert_2 , \label{eqn:F}\\
O &= \max_{x', x'' \in B} \lVert \Phi_s(x') - \Phi_s(x'') \rVert_2 . \label{eqn:O}
\end{align}
Here $L=\sqrt{\mathcal{L}(x)}$ is the worst-case distance to the clean teacher target, $F$ is the
clean fidelity error, and $O$ is the diameter of the student's feature set $\Phi_s(B)$. Thus, $F$
measures departure from the teacher's clean representation, whereas $O$ measures local feature
variation independently of the teacher.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:decomp}
For any $x\in\mathcal{X}$ and $\epsilon\ge0$, the quantities defined above satisfy
\begin{equation}
L \;\le\; F + O \;\le\; 3L .
\label{eqn:decomp}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any $x'\in B$, the triangle inequality gives
\begin{equation*}
\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2
\le\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x)\rVert_2+\lVert\Phi_s(x)-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2
\le O+F.
\end{equation*}
Taking the maximum over $x'$ yields $L\le F+O$. Conversely, $F\le L$ because $x\in B$, and
for any $x',x''\in B$,
\begin{equation*}
\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x'')\rVert_2
\le\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2+\lVert\Phi_s(x'')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2
\le2L.
\end{equation*}
Hence $O\le2L$, which together with $F\le L$ gives $F+O\le3L$.
\end{proof}

The bound explains how a single anchor can serve both fidelity and stability: for any
$\delta\ge0$, $\mathcal{L}(x)\le\delta^2$ implies $F\le\delta$ and $O\le2\delta$.
Thus, keeping the anchor small limits both clean disagreement with the teacher and variation
among the student's perturbed features, without a coefficient between separate penalties.
Importantly, this control does not assume that the teacher's own features are stable under
perturbations. It follows from constraining the student around the clean target, rather than
from a bound on the teacher's local variation. The scope of this statement is discussed in
\Cref{app:anchor_scope}.

Because the anchor aligns the student with the teacher's feature representation, retaining the
teacher's classifier provides a direct way to convert this alignment into predictions.
With the shared head $h_t(z)=W_tz+b_t$, every $x'\in B$ satisfies
$\lVert f_s(x')-f_t(x)\rVert_2\le\lVert W_t\rVert_{2\to2}L$, where
$\lVert W_t\rVert_{2\to2}$ is the spectral norm. The anchor therefore also bounds departure
from the teacher's clean logits under this classifier, although preserving the predicted class
additionally depends on the teacher's logit margins. Together, these observations motivate
training the student feature extractor against the clean-feature anchor while leaving the
teacher's classifier unchanged. \Cref{sec:method} specifies the resulting optimization procedure
and extends it to sample-dependent perturbation radii.
