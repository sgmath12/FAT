\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Preliminaries}

Let $f=h\circ\Phi$ be a classifier with feature map $\Phi$ and linear head $h$. Adversarial training
\citep{PGD} solves
\begin{equation}
\min_\theta\ \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)}\ \max_{x'\in B(x,\epsilon)}\ \ell\big(f_\theta(x'),y\big),
\qquad B(x,\epsilon)=\{x':\lVert x'-x\rVert_\infty\le\epsilon\}.
\label{eqn:at}
\end{equation}
The supervisory signal is the label, and it is asserted uniformly over $B(x,\epsilon)$: every point
of the ball must reach full confidence in one class. That is where the accuracy cost comes from.
We write $\Phi_t$ for the frozen feature map of a network of the same architecture trained naturally on
the same data---the teacher throughout---and ask what it can supply.

\subsection{The Teacher Is Fragile, and Its Logits Are Worse Than No Teacher at All}
\label{sec:fragile}

The objection to a naturally trained teacher is not hypothetical. Under $\epsilon=8/255$ its own
feature rotates by $63.8^\circ$ and its norm inflates by $\times2.45$; its accuracy rests on
directions an adversary controls.

That fragility appears immediately if the teacher is consumed the way adversarial distillation
consumes one. \Cref{tab:natteacher} gives the published objectives with our natural teacher in
place of the robust teacher they assume, each at its own published recipe, against plain adversarial
training initialized from the same teacher.

  \centering
  \caption{Published adversarial-distillation objectives given a \emph{naturally} trained teacher.
  CIFAR-100, ResNet-18, 100 epochs, each method at its published recipe. Every one lands below simply
  not distilling.}
  \label{tab:natteacher}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrr}
    \toprule
    & clean & AA & NRR \\
    \midrule
    ARD \citep{ard}   & 57.61 & 20.24 & 29.96 \\
    RSLAD \citep{rslad}          & 59.68 & 21.30 & 31.40 \\
    AdaAD \citep{adaad}         & 59.79 & 23.19 & 33.42 \\
    PGD-AT, initialized at the teacher      & 57.73 & \textbf{26.46} & \textbf{36.29} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

None of it is a temperature that was left untuned: swept in a fixed base regime, the logit target
peaks at $24.48$ AutoAttack ($\tau=4$) and the curve is unimodal because $\tau$ is squeezed from both
sides---at $\tau=1$ the teacher's maximum probability is $0.820$ against the student's $0.016$, and at
$\tau=16$ the target is within $1\%$ of uniform.

\paragraph{Two defects, and they separate.} A method can go wrong in \emph{where} it reads the teacher
and in \emph{what} it reads from it, and \Cref{tab:natteacher} confounds them. Holding the read
point fixed at $x$ and changing only what is read---the teacher's logits against its features, same
regime, same attack, same teacher---gives $24.48\to\mathbf{25.88}$ AutoAttack. That isolates the
second defect.

The first is larger, and is visible where a method reads the teacher \emph{off} the clean point. IGDM
\citep{igdm} matches a finite difference $f_T(x+\delta)-f_T(x-\delta)$ as a stand-in for
the teacher's input gradient, which is a gradient only while the teacher is locally linear; its own
diagnostic reports a Taylor remainder proportion of $0.012$ for adversarially trained teachers.
Applying that diagnostic to our checkpoints gives $0.0111$ for an adversarially trained
ResNet-18---reproducing their value, so the measurement is calibrated---and $\mathbf{0.4763}$ for the
natural teacher, forty times further from linear. The target it constructs is correspondingly
degenerate: $\lVert f_T(x+\delta)-f_T(x-\delta)\rVert=14.14$ against $\lVert f_T(x)\rVert=18.36$, with
the softmax of that difference placing $0.917$ on a single class.

The teacher therefore holds accuracy that adversarial training gives up, and the logit route does not
recover it. What follows keeps the read point at $x$, where the first defect cannot arise, and takes
the features, which the isolation above shows to be the better read.

\subsection{What a Clean-Feature Anchor Controls}
\label{sec:decomp}

Consider matching the teacher's feature at the \emph{clean} input against the student's feature at the
\emph{adversarial} input. Fix $x$, write $B=B(x,\epsilon)$, and let
\begin{equation}
L=\max_{x'\in B}\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert,\quad
F=\lVert\Phi_s(x)-\Phi_t(x)\rVert,\quad
O=\max_{x',x''\in B}\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x'')\rVert,
\label{eqn:lfo}
\end{equation}
so $L$ is that quantity, $F$ is fidelity to the teacher at the clean point, and $O$ is the oscillation
of the student's own map over the ball.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:decomp}
$L\le F+O\le 3L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$F\le L$ since $x\in B$; $O\le 2L$ by inserting $\Phi_t(x)$; and
$\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert\le\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x)\rVert+F\le O+F$, so $L\le F+O$.
\end{proof}

A single term therefore controls fidelity and local stability together, within a factor of three, with
no coefficient trading them off---the first is what adversarial training gives up, the second what its
label term buys. The proof uses only the triangle inequality, so it holds for the networks we train.

Two consequences decide the design. First, $\Phi_t$ is evaluated at $x$ alone, so the instability
measured in \Cref{sec:fragile} never enters the objective---and, symmetrically, the teacher
cannot supply robustness. Second, the decomposition holds only when the anchor \emph{is} the
objective: added to a cross-entropy term it characterizes nothing.

Both are verifiable, and we verify them. On the trained student over $\epsilon=8/255$ balls,
$L=7.58$, $F=6.30$ and $O_{\mathrm{lb}}=2.16$ against the teacher's own $O=6.68$: the student is
$3.1\times$ more stable than the teacher it was distilled from, and what remains in the loss is
fidelity rather than instability.
