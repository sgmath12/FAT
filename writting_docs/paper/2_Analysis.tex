\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

A natural teacher's clean accuracy need not indicate how well it will guide a robust student.
We first establish a simple regression target and characterize what it controls, then examine
how teacher selection changes the student's clean--robust trade-off. This separates the choice of
supervisory target from the choice of teacher that supplies it.

\subsection{Preliminaries}

Let $x\in\mathcal X=[0,1]^D$ and
$B(x,\epsilon)=\{x'\in\mathcal X:\lVert x'-x\rVert_\infty\le\epsilon\}$. We decompose a network as
$f=h\circ\Phi$, where $\Phi:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^d$ is a continuous feature map and
$h:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^C$, $h(z)=Wz+b$, is the classifier for $C$ classes. We use subscripts
$t$ and $s$ for the frozen natural teacher and the student. Both networks share the same architecture,
with fixed network statistics for the analysis; feature distances are Euclidean unless stated otherwise.

\subsection{Do Natural Teachers Need Softened Targets?}
\label{sec:fragile}

Can the natural teacher's concentrated clean predictions provide effective adversarial supervision
if softened? Motivated by soft-label robust distillation \citep{ard,rslad,adr,bmtard}, we first vary
target concentration before changing the representation being transferred. Let
\begin{equation}
q_t^\tau(x)=\operatorname{softmax}(f_t(x)/\tau),\qquad
p_s^\tau(x')=\operatorname{softmax}(f_s(x')/\tau).
\label{eqn:soft_targets}
\end{equation}
We compare softening only the teacher target with conventional shared-temperature KD:
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t^\tau(x)\|p_s^1(x')),\qquad
\tau^2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t^\tau(x)\|p_s^\tau(x')).
\label{eqn:kd_variants}
\end{equation}
As $\tau\to\infty$, the teacher target approaches a uniform distribution in both cases, but the
scaled shared-temperature loss retains logit information. For fixed logits and
$\bar f=f-C^{-1}\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top f$,
\begin{equation}
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\tau^2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t^\tau(x)\|p_s^\tau(x'))
=\frac{1}{2C}\lVert\bar f_t(x)-\bar f_s(x')\rVert_2^2.
\label{eqn:kd_limit}
\end{equation}
This limit motivates direct regression onto clean teacher logits; we also examine regression onto
the full clean feature, which defines
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{anchor}}(x)=
\max_{x'\in B(x,\epsilon)}\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2^2.
\label{eqn:anchor_percell}
\end{equation}

We train for 50 epochs at $\epsilon=8/255$, without weight averaging or AWP.
Across the full sweep, teacher-only softening improves robustness until the target becomes nearly
uniform, but changes clean accuracy little. Shared-temperature KD avoids this turnover and approaches
logit regression (\Cref{fig:targetcomparison}). Feature regression is only $0.13$ clean and $0.19$
AA$_2$ points higher: both regression targets are effective without temperature selection, but this
does not establish feature superiority. Moreover, KD updates the classifier whereas regression uses
a fixed head, so this comparison evaluates training designs rather than isolating the target alone.

\label{sec:sole_target}
These results support direct regression to clean teacher targets in either space.
We use features to preserve the representation supplied to the inherited classifier, including
directions that logit regression need not constrain, and next examine what this single target controls.

\subsection{What the Anchor Controls}
\label{sec:decomp}

The anchor provides a common reference for clean fidelity and local variation. Let
$x\in\mathcal X$ and $B=B(x,\epsilon)$, and define
\begin{equation}
L=\max_{x'\in B}\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2,\quad
F=\lVert\Phi_s(x)-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2,\quad
O=\max_{x',x''\in B}\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x'')\rVert_2.
\label{eqn:LFO}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-anchor decomposition]
\label{prop:decomp}
For every $x\in\mathcal X$ and $\epsilon\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
L\le F+O\le3L.
\label{eqn:decomp}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any $x'\in B$, the triangle inequality gives
$\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2\le
\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x)\rVert_2+F\le O+F$; maximizing over $x'$ yields $L\le F+O$.
Moreover, $x\in B$ implies $F\le L$, and, for any $x',x''\in B$, applying the triangle inequality
through $\Phi_t(x)$ gives $\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x'')\rVert_2\le2L$. Hence $O\le2L$ and
$F+O\le3L$.
\end{proof}

If $L\le\delta$, then the clean
student feature is within $\delta$ of the teacher and the student's feature diameter over the ball is
at most $2\delta$, without assuming that the teacher itself is stable. The inherited linear head also
bounds logit deviation by $\lVert W_t\rVert_{2\rightarrow2}L$, although classification depends on
the teacher's clean margin. The same argument applies to norm-based logit regression: it explains
joint control by a fixed target, not a feature-specific advantage. Finite-step PGD does not certify $L$.

\subsection{Does Teacher Accuracy Predict Transfer?}
\label{sec:teacher_transfer}
\label{sec:geometry}

The bound above describes training against a given target, but does not identify which teacher
to choose. A natural criterion is the teacher's clean accuracy. To test it, we vary the checkpoint
along one natural-training trajectory while keeping the student training procedure fixed. Each
checkpoint supplies the initialization, feature targets, and inherited classifier; the experiment
therefore evaluates teacher selection as a whole, rather than changing the targets alone.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figure/teacher_checkpoint_trends.pdf}
  \caption{Teacher checkpoint sweep on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18). (a) Accuracy changes relative to the
  50-epoch teacher. (b) Within-to-between scatter ratios $S_w/S_b$; lower means stronger class
  separation, and labels denote teacher epochs. Lines are least-squares fits. Exact values are in
  \Cref{tab:teacherladder_values}.}
  \label{fig:teacherladder}
\end{figure}

Across the sweep, the teacher gains $2.51$ clean points while the student loses $2.04$;
student AA$_2$ nevertheless rises from $24.38\%$ to $25.80\%$ (\Cref{fig:teacherladder}a).
The more accurate teacher thus shifts the student toward robustness rather than improving both
accuracies. Selecting the checkpoint with the highest teacher clean accuracy would miss this
trade-off.

To examine what changes alongside accuracy, we measure $S_w/S_b$ on unit-normalized clean features:
$S_w$ is the mean squared distance to the corresponding class mean, and $S_b$ is the mean squared
distance of class means to the global mean. Teacher and student ratios decrease together
(\Cref{fig:teacherladder}b), indicating stronger class separation. This is an association across
five checkpoints, not evidence that geometry alone causes the transfer effect.

Correlations along one trajectory cannot separate a teacher property from training time, so we test
the criterion directly on teachers trained differently. Label smoothing and mixup give two natural
teachers whose clean accuracies differ by $0.14$ points and whose students differ by $4.18$
(\Cref{tab:matchedteacher}). Their AutoAttack accuracies agree to $0.02$: at matched teacher accuracy
the clean axis moves and the robust axis does not. Of the teacher-only quantities we measured, the one
that orders the pair is the feature sensitivity
$\lVert\Phi_t(x+\delta)-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2/\lVert\delta\rVert_2$, which uses neither labels nor logits;
a line fit on the nine trajectory checkpoints predicts $59.0$ and $65.0$ from it, against $64.3$ and
$64.2$ from teacher clean accuracy, which cannot tell the two teachers apart.

The rule does not extend to every teacher. The weight-decay teacher has the sensitivity of the
200-epoch one and gives a student $7.42$ clean points below it, so sensitivity is a candidate
explanation for differences among teachers of comparable accuracy rather than a selector. The margin
and separation metrics, which correlate most strongly along the trajectory, fail outside it;
\Cref{app:teachersel} reports those predictions, the full metric set and the retracted checkpoint.
What survives is that teachers of equal accuracy transfer differently, so checkpoints have to be
assessed by the student trade-off they produce. Our subsequent experiments use the 200-epoch teacher,
which lies on the late-checkpoint robustness plateau while retaining more student clean accuracy than
the 300-epoch teacher. Once the teacher is fixed, a separate question remains: how should the
perturbation budget be distributed across examples whose anchor losses have different input
sensitivities? We address this next.
