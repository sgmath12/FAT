\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

A natural teacher offers accurate clean predictions, but these need not be the most useful targets
for adversarial training. We compare predictive and feature targets, establish what a fixed clean
target controls, and then ask how the teacher checkpoint changes the student's behavior.

\subsection{Preliminaries}

Let $x\in\mathcal X=[0,1]^D$ and
$B(x,\epsilon)=\{x'\in\mathcal X:\lVert x'-x\rVert_\infty\le\epsilon\}$. We decompose a network as
$f=h\circ\Phi$, where $\Phi:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^d$ is a continuous feature map and
$h:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^C$, $h(z)=Wz+b$, is the classifier for $C$ classes. We use subscripts
$t$ and $s$ for the frozen natural teacher and the student. Both networks share the same architecture,
with fixed network statistics for the analysis; feature distances are Euclidean unless stated otherwise.

\subsection{Do Natural Teachers Need Softened Targets?}
\label{sec:fragile}

Can the natural teacher's concentrated clean predictions provide effective adversarial supervision
if softened? Motivated by soft-label robust distillation \citep{ard,rslad,adr,bmtard}, we first vary
target concentration before changing the representation being transferred. Let
\begin{equation}
q_t^\tau(x)=\operatorname{softmax}(f_t(x)/\tau),\qquad
p_s^\tau(x')=\operatorname{softmax}(f_s(x')/\tau).
\label{eqn:soft_targets}
\end{equation}
We compare softening only the teacher target with conventional shared-temperature KD:
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t^\tau(x)\|p_s^1(x')),\qquad
\tau^2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t^\tau(x)\|p_s^\tau(x')).
\label{eqn:kd_variants}
\end{equation}
As $\tau\to\infty$, the teacher target approaches a uniform distribution in both cases, but the
scaled shared-temperature loss retains logit information. For fixed logits and
$\bar f=f-C^{-1}\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top f$,
\begin{equation}
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\tau^2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t^\tau(x)\|p_s^\tau(x'))
=\frac{1}{2C}\lVert\bar f_t(x)-\bar f_s(x')\rVert_2^2.
\label{eqn:kd_limit}
\end{equation}
This limit motivates direct regression onto clean teacher logits; we also examine regression onto
the full clean feature, which defines
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{anchor}}(x)=
\max_{x'\in B(x,\epsilon)}\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2^2.
\label{eqn:anchor_percell}
\end{equation}

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figure/analysis_targets.pdf}
  \caption{Target comparison on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18). (a) Teacher-target entropy approaches the
  uniform value as $\tau$ increases. (b) Nondominated settings within each
  KD variant, alongside fixed-head logit and feature regression. The full temperature sweep is in
  \Cref{tab:targetcomparison_values}.}
  \label{fig:targetcomparison}
\end{figure}

We train for 50 epochs at $\epsilon=8/255$.
Across the full sweep, teacher-only softening improves robustness until the target becomes nearly
uniform, but changes clean accuracy little. Shared-temperature KD avoids this turnover and approaches
logit regression (\Cref{fig:targetcomparison}). Feature regression is only $0.13$ clean and $0.19$
AA points higher: both regression targets are effective without temperature selection. Moreover,
KD updates the classifier whereas regression uses a fixed head, so this comparison evaluates
training designs rather than isolating the target alone.

\label{sec:sole_target}
The comparison points to a fixed clean target rather than a particular temperature. We use the
full feature to preserve the representation supplied to the inherited classifier, including
directions that logit regression need not constrain. The small gap above does not establish a
feature-specific performance advantage; it motivates examining what the single anchor controls.

\subsection{What the Anchor Controls}
\label{sec:decomp}

The fixed target links agreement with the teacher at the clean input to variation of the student's
features around it. To make this relation precise, let
$x\in\mathcal X$ and $B=B(x,\epsilon)$, and define
\begin{equation}
L=\max_{x'\in B}\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2,\quad
F=\lVert\Phi_s(x)-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2,\quad
O=\max_{x',x''\in B}\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x'')\rVert_2.
\label{eqn:LFO}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-anchor decomposition]
\label{prop:decomp}
For every $x\in\mathcal X$ and $\epsilon\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
L\le F+O\le3L.
\label{eqn:decomp}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any $x'\in B$, the triangle inequality gives
$\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert_2\le
\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x)\rVert_2+F\le O+F$; maximizing over $x'$ yields $L\le F+O$.
Moreover, $x\in B$ implies $F\le L$, and, for any $x',x''\in B$, applying the triangle inequality
through $\Phi_t(x)$ gives $\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x'')\rVert_2\le2L$. Hence $O\le2L$ and
$F+O\le3L$.
\end{proof}

If $L\le\delta$, then the clean
student feature is within $\delta$ of the teacher and the student's feature diameter over the ball is
at most $2\delta$, without assuming that the teacher itself is stable. The inherited linear head also
bounds logit deviation by $\lVert W_t\rVert_{2\rightarrow2}L$, although classification depends on
the teacher's clean margin. The same argument applies to norm-based logit regression: it explains
joint control by a fixed target, not a feature-specific advantage. Finite-step PGD does not certify $L$.

\subsection{How Does the Teacher Checkpoint Affect Transfer?}
\label{sec:teacher_transfer}
\label{sec:geometry}

The bound holds for any fixed target, but cannot say which teacher will produce the best student.
The most obvious choice is the checkpoint with the highest clean accuracy. We test this by training
the same student procedure from different checkpoints of a natural teacher. Each checkpoint provides
the initialization, feature target, and classifier, so the comparison measures their combined effect.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figure/teacher_checkpoint_trends.pdf}
  \caption{Teacher checkpoint sweep on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18). (a) Accuracy changes relative to the
  50-epoch teacher. (b) Within-to-between scatter ratios $S_w/S_b$; lower means stronger class
  separation, and labels denote teacher epochs. Lines are least-squares fits. Exact values are in
  \Cref{tab:teacherladder_values}.}
  \label{fig:teacherladder}
\end{figure}

From 50 to 300 teacher epochs, teacher clean accuracy gains $2.51$ points, whereas student clean
accuracy falls by $2.04$ and student AA rises from $24.38\%$ to $25.80\%$
(\Cref{fig:teacherladder}a). The later checkpoint does not improve both student accuracies: it
moves the operating point toward robustness. Nor is the shift strictly monotone; the 200-epoch
student has slightly higher AA than the 300-epoch student.

An independently trained teacher trajectory shows the same pattern: from 40 to 300 epochs, teacher
clean accuracy rises from $75.57\%$ to $78.27\%$, while student clean accuracy falls from $64.36\%$
to $62.34\%$ and AA rises from $23.86\%$ to $25.94\%$ (\Cref{tab:teachertrajectory}). Both
trajectories therefore reproduce a change in the student's trade-off, rather than a rule that a
later checkpoint is always better.

To examine what accompanies this shift, we measure the within-to-between scatter ratio $S_w/S_b$
on unit-normalized clean features. Teacher and student ratios decrease together
(\Cref{fig:teacherladder}b), consistent with stronger class separation. This association does not
identify which change in the teacher causes the student shift.

The checkpoint sweep entangles training stage and teacher accuracy. To see whether accuracy alone
describes transfer, we also compare a label-smoothed and a mixup teacher with nearly equal clean
accuracy. Their students differ by $4.18$ clean points but only $0.02$ AA points
(\Cref{tab:matchedteacher}). Thus even at a similar teacher accuracy, the inherited representation
can lead to a different student. The teachers' feature sensitivities differ in the same order as
student clean accuracy, but one matched pair cannot establish a general predictor.

These results make teacher accuracy an incomplete guide to student performance, not a checkpoint
selection rule. We use the 200-epoch teacher for subsequent experiments and turn to a choice that
remains even when the teacher is fixed: the same pixel radius need not change the anchor loss equally
across examples.
