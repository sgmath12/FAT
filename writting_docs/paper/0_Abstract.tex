\begin{abstract}
Adversarial training improves adversarial robustness, but it does so at a cost in standard accuracy.
Adversarial distillation reduces this cost by guiding the student with an adversarially trained
teacher, whose robustness has to be obtained beforehand and at greater expense than training the
student itself. In contrast to these approaches, we guide the student with a naturally trained
network, a teacher that costs nothing adversarial to obtain, and ask it only for the standard accuracy
it holds, leaving robustness to the student's own inner maximization. Existing methods read a teacher
through its logits, and we find that the published distillation objectives all fall below plain
adversarial training when given a natural one. In this paper we propose Clean Feature Anchoring (CFA),
which anchors the student's adversarial feature to the teacher's clean feature. We prove that this
single term controls fidelity to the teacher and local stability of the student together within a
constant factor, so no coefficient is needed to balance them, and we pair it with a per-sample attack
radius set by the input sensitivity of the loss at a fixed total budget. The method requires no
temperature, no loss weight and no trained classifier, and one configuration is used on every dataset.
Experimental results show that CFA reaches $62.17\%$ standard accuracy at $28.86\%$ AutoAttack
accuracy on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, where no published method is ahead on both axes, and transfers
unchanged to CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet.
\end{abstract}
