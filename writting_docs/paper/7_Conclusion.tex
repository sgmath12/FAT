\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Adversarial training discards standard accuracy that a naturally trained copy of the same network
still holds. We asked whether self-distillation can put it back without touching where the robustness
comes from, and found that it can, provided the teacher is asked only for accuracy and is read only at
the clean point. Anchoring the student's adversarial feature to the teacher's clean feature makes that
precise: the teacher never enters the perturbation ball, so its own instability---$63.8^\circ$ of
rotation under $\epsilon = 8/255$---has no path into the objective, and the single term bounds fidelity
and local stability together to within a factor of three. The result is a method with no loss weight,
no temperature and no trained classifier, whose robustness remains entirely attributable to the
student's own inner maximization, and which transfers across three datasets with only the teacher
checkpoint changed.

Two findings sit alongside it. Consuming the same natural teacher the way adversarial distillation
does---as a logit target, or read inside the ball---is worse than not distilling at all, on both
datasets and for all four published objectives. And what the teacher transfers is its class geometry
rather than its accuracy, with the two anti-correlated at $r = -0.999$ across training lengths, which
turns the teacher's own schedule into a trade-off control that costs nothing to exercise.

What we do not have is an account of how much robustness the objective buys. \Cref{prop:decomp}
explains why anchoring a non-robust teacher is \emph{free}; it is not a robustness bound, and the two
partial results in \Cref{app:robustness} each fall short for a reason we state. Closing that is the
obvious next step, and the measurements that would constrain it---the oscillation $O$ correlating with
AutoAttack at the wrong sign, and the $\varepsilon$-independence above the threshold---are reported
here rather than set aside.
