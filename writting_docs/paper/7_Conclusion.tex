\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Effective transfer from a natural teacher depends on more than its clean accuracy: in the checkpoint
comparisons studied here, a more accurate teacher can yield lower student clean accuracy while
improving robustness. CFA makes that trade-off easy to examine, since one fixed clean feature target
guides both attack generation and backbone learning, and sensitivity-based radius allocation matches
the perturbation budget to the loss being optimized. Across three datasets the result is competitive
without a robust teacher and without a label objective, which motivates evaluating teachers by the
student trade-off they produce.
