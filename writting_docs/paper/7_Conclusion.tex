\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Effective transfer from a natural teacher depends on more than its clean accuracy. In the checkpoint
comparisons studied here, a more accurate teacher can yield lower student clean accuracy while
improving robustness. CFA provides a simple setting for examining this trade-off: a fixed clean
feature target guides both attack generation and backbone learning. For a chosen teacher,
sensitivity-based radius allocation adjusts the perturbation budget to the loss being optimized;
matched-distribution controls support the importance of which examples receive each radius.
Across three datasets, the resulting method is competitive without a robust teacher or an additional
label objective. The findings motivate evaluating teachers by their student trade-offs and matching
training perturbations to the supervisory target, rather than treating either choice as incidental.
