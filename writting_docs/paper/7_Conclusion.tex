\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Effective transfer from a natural teacher depends on more than its clean accuracy: in the checkpoint
comparisons studied here, a more accurate teacher can give a less accurate student while improving
robustness. CFA makes that trade-off easy to examine, since one fixed clean feature target drives both
attack generation and backbone learning, and the sensitivity-matched radius then matches the
perturbation budget to the loss being optimized. Across three datasets the result is competitive with
neither a robust teacher nor a label objective, which argues for choosing teachers by the student
trade-off they produce.
