\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental Settings}

\paragraph{Datasets.} CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 \citep{krizhevsky2009learning} and Tiny-ImageNet-200
\citep{le2015tiny}. Random crop with 4-pixel padding and random horizontal flip; no other
augmentation.

\paragraph{Teacher.} For each dataset, a network of the same architecture as the student, trained
naturally for 200 epochs on the same data (Table~\ref{tab:teachers}). No adversarial training is
performed at any point in the teacher's construction.

\paragraph{Training details.} ResNet-18 throughout the main tables, WideResNet-34-10 in
Section~\ref{sec:wrn}. The student is initialized from the teacher and trained for 100 epochs with
AdamW at learning rate $0.021$ under a one-cycle schedule, batch size $128$. The attack is 10-step PGD
at step size $2/255$ with a training radius of $8.8/255$; Section~\ref{sec:radius} varies that radius.
Weight averaging ($\kappa=0.999$, from $20\%$ of the run) and an AWP proxy \citep{wu2020adversarial}
($\gamma=0.005$, after epoch $10$) are used and ablated in Section~\ref{sec:components}. Every dataset
uses this identical configuration; only the dataset name and the teacher checkpoint change.

\paragraph{Comparison models.} Two groups: adversarial training methods requiring no external network
(PGD-AT, TRADES, MART, Consistency-AT, RPAT, ADR, CURE, Generalist++, ARREST), and adversarial
distillation methods, which assume an adversarially trained teacher (ARD, RSLAD, AdaAD, IGDM,
B-MTARD). Published numbers are quoted with their source; RPAT and CURE we additionally re-ran, and
the four distillation objectives we ported and ran on our own natural teacher
(Section~\ref{sec:natteacher}).

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Clean accuracy, FGSM, PGD-20/10/50, CW$_\infty$ and AutoAttack (standard
version) on the full test set at $\varepsilon=8/255$. NRR is the harmonic mean of clean and AutoAttack
accuracy. All of our numbers are the final-epoch weight-averaged model, with no best-checkpoint
selection.

\paragraph{Reproductions.} We reproduce RPAT++ to within $0.31$ AutoAttack of its published
PreActResNet-18 numbers on both datasets and report our own measurement. CURE we could \emph{not}
reproduce: seven runs of the official repository fall short on \emph{different} axes---the closest on
standard accuracy reaches $86.11$ at AA $40.65$, the closest on robustness AA $45.63$ at clean
$81.15$, while the published pair has both. Computing its RGP prominence mask on absolute values moves
AA by $+15.9$, which localizes the discrepancy without closing it, so the CURE row above is the
paper's own number.

The sensitivity rule contributes exactly $+0.49$ NRR at both schedule lengths ($+1.61/{+}0.21$ at 50

exceeds ADR's full stack ($38.46$ against $38.08$), at half the schedule and one fewer component, so
the gain is not the stack.

Two things hold on both datasets. NRR peaks at $8.8/255$, but the curve is mild and the standard
$8/255$ already exceeds the best published result on either dataset ($38.72$ against ADR's $38.08$;
$64.48$ against CURE's $63.19$), so the non-standard radius is a refinement rather than the source of
the result. And past $8.8/255$ the radius buys nothing: AutoAttack and CW are identical to the last
decimal at $10/255$ while standard accuracy falls $2.66$ and $2.29$, matching the
$\varepsilon$-independence Appendix~\ref{app:robustness} predicts above the threshold.

Here $r(\text{teacher clean},\ \text{student clean}) = -0.999$: the teacher gains $2.51$ points across
the ladder and the student \emph{loses} $2.04$. NRR peaks in the middle, so the teacher's training
length is a control on the operating point, and locating it costs five natural-training runs and no
adversarial ones. The effect reproduces on Tiny-ImageNet, where it is accuracy-controlled: two
teachers $0.32$ apart move the student by $1.92$ clean and $1.58$ AA.
