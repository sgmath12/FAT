\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Preliminaries}

Let $f=h\circ\Phi$ be a classifier with feature map $\Phi$ and linear head $h$. Adversarial training
\citep{madry2018towards} solves
\begin{equation}
\min_\theta\ \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)}\ \max_{x'\in B(x,\varepsilon)}\ \ell\big(f_\theta(x'),y\big),
\qquad B(x,\varepsilon)=\{x':\lVert x'-x\rVert_\infty\le\varepsilon\}.
\label{eq:at}
\end{equation}
The supervisory signal is the label, and it is asserted uniformly over $B(x,\varepsilon)$: every point
of the ball must reach full confidence in one class. That is where the accuracy cost comes from.
Adversarial distillation replaces the label with a teacher quantity, taken from the logits of an
adversarially trained teacher---at $x$ \citep{goldblum2020adversarially,zi2021revisiting}, at
$x_{\mathrm{adv}}$ \citep{huang2023boosting}, or from finite differences of both
\citep{lee2025indirect}. We ask instead what a \emph{naturally} trained teacher, which costs nothing
to obtain, can supply.

\subsection{The Teacher Is Fragile, and Its Logits Are Worse Than No Teacher at All}
\label{sec:fragile}

The objection to a naturally trained teacher is not hypothetical. Under $\varepsilon=8/255$ its own
feature rotates by $63.8^\circ$ and its norm inflates by $\times2.45$; its accuracy rests on
directions an adversary controls.

That fragility appears immediately if the teacher is consumed the way adversarial distillation
consumes one. Table~\ref{tab:natteacher} gives the published objectives with our natural teacher in
place of the robust teacher they assume, each at its own published recipe, against plain adversarial
training initialized from the same teacher.

The same holds when the temperature is swept rather than inherited: in a fixed base regime the best
logit target reaches $24.48$ AutoAttack at $\tau=4$, against $25.88$ for a feature target under
identical conditions, and the logit curve is unimodal because $\tau$ is squeezed from both sides---at
$\tau=1$ the teacher's maximum probability is $0.820$ against the student's $0.016$, and at $\tau=16$
the target is within $1\%$ of uniform.

The teacher therefore holds accuracy that adversarial training gives up, and the logit route does not
recover it. What follows asks what does.

\subsection{What a Clean-Feature Anchor Controls}
\label{sec:decomp}

Consider matching the teacher's feature at the \emph{clean} input against the student's feature at the
\emph{adversarial} input. Fix $x$, write $B=B(x,\varepsilon)$, and let
\begin{equation}
L=\max_{x'\in B}\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert,\quad
F=\lVert\Phi_s(x)-\Phi_t(x)\rVert,\quad
O=\max_{x',x''\in B}\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x'')\rVert,
\label{eq:LFO}
\end{equation}
so $L$ is that quantity, $F$ is fidelity to the teacher at the clean point, and $O$ is the oscillation
of the student's own map over the ball.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:decomp}
$L\le F+O\le 3L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$F\le L$ since $x\in B$; $O\le 2L$ by inserting $\Phi_t(x)$; and
$\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\rVert\le\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_s(x)\rVert+F\le O+F$, so $L\le F+O$.
\end{proof}

A single term therefore controls fidelity and local stability together, within a factor of three, with
no coefficient trading them off---the first is what adversarial training gives up, the second what its
label term buys. The proof uses only the triangle inequality, so it holds for the networks we train.

Two consequences decide the design. First, $\Phi_t$ is evaluated at $x$ alone, so the instability
measured in Section~\ref{sec:fragile} never enters the objective---and, symmetrically, the teacher
cannot supply robustness. Second, the decomposition holds only when the anchor \emph{is} the
objective: added to a cross-entropy term it characterizes nothing.

Both are verifiable, and we verify them. On the trained student over $\varepsilon=8/255$ balls,
$L=7.58$, $F=6.30$ and $O_{\mathrm{lb}}=2.16$ against the teacher's own $O=6.68$: the student is
$3.1\times$ more stable than the teacher it was distilled from, and what remains in the loss is
fidelity rather than instability.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Clean Feature Anchoring}

Let $\Phi_t$ be the frozen feature map of a network of the same architecture trained naturally on the
same data. The student is initialized at $\Phi_s=\Phi_t$ and trained with
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}_x\big\lVert\Phi_s(x_{\mathrm{adv}})-\Phi_t(x)\big\rVert^2,
\qquad
x_{\mathrm{adv}}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x'\in B(x,\varepsilon)}\big\lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x)\big\rVert.
\label{eq:anchor}
\end{equation}
The head is never trained: at test time the student is $h_t\circ\Phi_s$. Writing the target on the
feature is what makes this possible---a logit loss constrains $W\Phi$ and so determines $(\Phi,W)$
only up to $(A\Phi,WA^{-1})$, while a feature target pins the representation itself, so the teacher's
classifier remains calibrated for it. Two further properties are worth naming: \eqref{eq:anchor} has
no weight and no temperature, being the whole objective rather than a term added to another; and it is
exactly zero at initialization, so every gradient the backbone receives is attributable to the attack.

\subsection{Sensitivity-Matched $\varepsilon$}
\label{sec:angeps}

A fixed pixel radius is not a fixed amount of pressure on the objective. Within an $\ell_\infty$ ball
of radius $e$,
\begin{equation}
\max_{\lVert\delta\rVert_\infty\le e}\langle\nabla_x\mathcal{L},\delta\rangle
=e\,\lVert\nabla_x\mathcal{L}\rVert_1,
\label{eq:firstorder}
\end{equation}
so with $g_i=\lVert\nabla_x\mathcal{L}(x_i)\rVert$ the same radius moves the objective by amounts that
differ as $g_i$ does. Equalizing that movement at fixed total budget $\sum_i\varepsilon_i=N\varepsilon$
gives
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_i\propto g_i^{-p},\qquad
\varepsilon_i\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}\big(\varepsilon_i,0.5\varepsilon,1.5\varepsilon\big),\qquad
\varepsilon_i\leftarrow\varepsilon_i\cdot N\varepsilon\Big/\sum\nolimits_j\varepsilon_j,
\label{eq:angeps}
\end{equation}
with $p=1$---equivalently the max--min allocation, since budget moved to the sample whose objective
moves least always improves the worst case. Budget preservation is what makes the comparison
interpretable: $p{=}1$ and $p{=}0$ spend the same total attack budget, so any difference between them
is allocation and not strength.

Per-sample radii are not new; IAAT, MMA and CAT assign one from the difficulty of the sample or its
margin in \emph{input} space. This rule assigns one from the input-sensitivity of the \emph{training
loss}, the geometry the objective is written in. Holding the exact multiset of weights
\eqref{eq:angeps} produces and only reassigning which sample receives which, by difficulty, pays
$0.74$ AutoAttack and falls below the uniform baseline on CW and NRR: the same weights in a different
order move \emph{along} the frontier, and only this order moves the frontier itself.

\subsection{Algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Clean Feature Anchoring (CFA)}
\label{alg:cfa}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require dataset $\mathcal{D}$, radius $\varepsilon$, steps $K$, exponent $p=1$
\State $\Phi_t \gets$ train $f_t$ naturally on $\mathcal{D}$
  \Comment{same architecture, no adversarial cost}
\State $\Phi_s \gets \Phi_t$, \ $h \gets h_t$ \Comment{student initialized at the teacher}
\For{each epoch, each minibatch $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$}
  \State $g_i \gets \big\lVert\nabla_x\lVert\Phi_s(x_i)-\Phi_t(x_i)\rVert^2\big\rVert$
    \Comment{one backward pass at $x$}
  \State $\varepsilon_i \gets \mathrm{clip}\big(\varepsilon(g_i/\bar g)^{-p},
    0.5\varepsilon, 1.5\varepsilon\big)$;\ \
    $\varepsilon_i \gets \varepsilon_i N\varepsilon/\sum_j\varepsilon_j$
    \Comment{budget preserved}
  \State $x_i^{\mathrm{adv}} \gets \mathrm{PGD}_K\big(\max_{x'\in B(x_i,\varepsilon_i)}
    \lVert\Phi_s(x')-\Phi_t(x_i)\rVert\big)$
  \State update $\Phi_s$ on
    $\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\lVert\Phi_s(x_i^{\mathrm{adv}})-\Phi_t(x_i)\rVert^2$
    \Comment{$h$ is never updated}
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Line~1 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:cfa} is the only place labels appear, and it is ordinary supervised
training; the remainder uses none.

\paragraph{Caveats.} Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp} bounds $F+O$, and $O$ is not a sufficient statistic
for robust accuracy---across the teacher ladder of Section~\ref{sec:geometry} it correlates with
AutoAttack at $r=+0.83$, the wrong sign. It explains why anchoring to a non-robust teacher is
\emph{free}, not how much robustness the objective buys; Appendix~\ref{app:robustness} gives two
partial results on the latter, each with the reason it falls short. In \eqref{eq:firstorder} the
derivation asks for the $\ell_1$ norm and our runs use $\ell_2$; both were trained and agree within
noise. Finally, $g_i$ is one backward pass at the clean $x_i$, so it is the sensitivity at the
attack's starting point rather than along it.
